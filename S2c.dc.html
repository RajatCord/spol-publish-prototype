How the score is built

Six parts, each worth a fixed share of 100. A part loses points only for its own findings, averaged over the standards it applies to, so no part can cost more than its share and no single standard more than its slice of a part. High findings count 3 points, Medium 1, Low none; 6 points empties a part for that standard. Each part is rounded down, so a part shows full marks only when it has nothing to fix, and the total is the sum you see.

See it the way a reviewer will

AI reads every selected standard the way a SACSCOC reviewer would and scores what the rules can't:

  • Argument strength: does each standard actually make its case?
  • Evidence sufficiency: which claims have nothing behind them.
  • Consistency: numbers and names that disagree between standards.
  • Clarity: passages a reviewer is likely to misread.
  • Reviewer readiness: which standards still carry a finding a committee would cite.
